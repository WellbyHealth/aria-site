The 13-week cash forecast: how practices stop worrying about payroll

How to build a simple 13-week cash forecast for a medical practice, and why it beats checking the bank balance the night before payroll.

By the MD CFO team · November 10, 2026

If you have ever checked your bank balance the night before payroll, you already know why a cash forecast matters. It is not about being fancy. It is about seeing a tight week three weeks before it arrives, instead of the night before.

A 13-week forecast is the standard tool for this. Thirteen weeks is one quarter: long enough to see trouble coming, short enough to stay accurate.

The simple version

Make a table with 13 columns, one per week. Then fill in four rows:

  • Starting cash for the week
  • Money coming in: insurer deposits, patient payments, anything else
  • Money going out: payroll, rent, supplies, loan payments, other bills
  • Ending cash, which becomes next week's starting cash

That is the whole thing. The hard part is not the math. It is being honest about when money actually arrives.

Use real timing, not wishful timing

Insurers do not pay the day you see the patient. Look at your last few months of deposits and work out how long each big payer usually takes. Use that delay in your forecast. Slow payers are the number one reason practices get surprised.

Mark payroll weeks clearly. In most practices payroll is the biggest outflow, and it does not wait.

Update it every week

A forecast you build once and never touch is just a guess with a date on it. Every week, replace the estimates for the week that just ended with what really happened, and roll the forecast forward one week.

After a month or two, you will trust it. That is the point where it stops being a spreadsheet and starts being a decision tool: when to order supplies, when to hold a purchase, when to draw on a line of credit and when you do not need to.

What good looks like

You should be able to answer one question at any time: what is our lowest cash point in the next 13 weeks, and when is it? If you can answer that, payroll stops being a worry.
