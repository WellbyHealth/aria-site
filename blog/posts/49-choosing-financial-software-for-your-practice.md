Choosing financial software for a medical practice is mostly about fit, not features. Demos are built to impress, and almost every tool looks good for forty-five minutes. What matters is how it works on a busy Tuesday, with your systems, your staff and your patients' data.

Here's what to focus on, and the questions that separate a good fit from a frustrating one.

## Start with the problem you're actually solving

Before you look at anything, write down the two or three problems you want fixed. Be specific. "Bills get paid late because approvals happen over text." "We don't know if payers are paying contract rates." "The books are three months behind."

Then judge every tool against that list. A product with fifty features you don't need and a weak version of the one you do isn't a good deal.

## Does it work with your practice management system and EHR?

Practice finance touches a lot of systems: your practice management system, EHR, clearinghouse, bank accounts, cards, payroll and accounting software. A tool that doesn't connect means someone on your staff becomes the integration, copying numbers by hand.

Ask:

- Which of our current systems does it connect to, and how? Automatic sync, file upload or manual entry?
- How often does data sync?
- What happens when a sync fails? Who notices, and how?
- Can we see a live connection to a system like ours, not just a slide?

## Security, HIPAA and who can see what

If a vendor will handle protected health information on your behalf, HIPAA generally requires a business associate agreement with them. Ask about it directly, early. The [HHS](https://www.hhs.gov) site has plain-language guidance on HIPAA if you want background, and your attorney can review the agreement itself.

Beyond that, ask about:

- Multi-factor sign-in for every user
- Role-based permissions, so a front desk user can't see or do what an owner can
- Audit logs that show who changed what and when
- What happens to your data if you cancel

## Approvals and controls for paying bills

Any tool that moves money should make it easy to control who can do what. Look for:

- Approval rules, such as requiring an owner sign-off over a set amount
- Separate roles for entering a bill and approving payment
- Spending limits by person, card or location
- A clear record of every approval

If a vendor treats approvals as an afterthought, that tells you something. (For a sense of what good [bill pay](https://ariapractice.com/bill-pay) controls look like, start there.)

## Can you trace every number back to its source?

Dashboards are only useful if you trust them. A good test: pick any number on the screen and ask how to see the transactions behind it. If nobody can show you, be careful.

Also ask how it handles corrections. When a transaction is miscategorized, how is it fixed, and does the fix flow through to reports?

## Who owns your data, and can you get it out?

Your financial history belongs to you. Before signing, find out:

- Can we export all our data, in a standard format, at any time?
- Is there a fee to export?
- How long is data kept after we leave?

Switching costs are real. Easy export keeps them manageable.

## What does implementation and support actually look like?

Ask who sets it up, how long it typically takes and how much staff time it needs from your side. Then ask about support after go-live. Is it email only? Is there a named person? What are the hours? Ask to speak with someone who'll actually support you, not just the salesperson.

## What's the total cost of financial software?

The subscription price is only part of it. Add up:

- Per-user or per-location fees
- Transaction or processing fees (for things like [patient payments](https://ariapractice.com/patient-payments))
- Setup or implementation fees
- Add-ons you'll need to get the features you were shown
- Staff time to learn it and run it

Get pricing in writing, including what happens at renewal.

## Red flags to watch for in a demo

- Vague answers about security or data handling
- No way to try it on your own data before committing
- Long contracts with no exit if it doesn't work
- Features that are "on the roadmap" but presented as if they exist

It's fair to ask any vendor, including us, to show you something working rather than describe it.
