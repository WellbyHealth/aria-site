If you've ever wondered what to send your bookkeeper each month, you're not alone. Plenty of practices pay for bookkeeping and still feel behind: the books close late, reports arrive and go unread, and questions pile up until tax season.

Often the bookkeeper isn't the problem. The handoff is. They can only work with what you send, and they can only answer what you ask.

## Set a monthly close date

Pick a date each month when everything is due to your bookkeeper. A few business days after month end works for most practices, once statements are out. Then agree on when the books will be closed and reports delivered.

Put both dates on the calendar. A close without a deadline just drifts.

## Monthly bookkeeping checklist: what to send

Most bookkeepers connect straight to your bank and card accounts, so they can see transactions. What they can't see is context. That's your part.

### Statements for every account

Even with live connections, send or confirm month-end statements for your operating and payroll accounts, business credit cards, loans and lines of credit, and any patient payment processor. Statements are what your bookkeeper reconciles against. If one's missing, the books aren't really closed.

### Receipts and notes for card purchases

Every card charge needs a receipt and a short note. "Lunch, staff training" or "Replacement otoscope, exam 3" heads off a round of emails. If receipts get collected during the month, this step is mostly done already.

### Vendor bills, paid and unpaid

Send the bills you got during the month and mark which are paid. Unpaid bills matter too. They show what you owe, not just what you spent.

### Payroll reports

Send the payroll summary for each pay period, including taxes and benefit deductions. Mention anyone who started or left.

### Revenue reports from your practice management system

Deposits show money coming in, not where it came from. Send your monthly reports on charges and collections, insurance payments by payer, patient payments, and adjustments and write-offs. These let your bookkeeper tie deposits back to the care you delivered.

### A short list of anything unusual

A new loan or equipment lease. A big one-time purchase. A refund to a patient or from a vendor. Money the owner put in or took out. A new bank account, provider or location. Two lines of explanation now will save you a long email thread later.

## Questions to ask your bookkeeper after the close

Once the books close, don't just file the reports away. Ask questions. You don't need an accounting degree for this.

### Is every account reconciled?

Ask for a plain yes or no, account by account. If something didn't reconcile, find out why and what's needed to fix it.

### What's still uncategorized?

Most months leave a few transactions nobody could identify. Get that list and clear it. Small unknowns turn into big ones if you leave them.

### How did this month compare?

Ask for a quick comparison with last month and the same month last year. You're looking for big moves in revenue, collections, payroll, supplies, or any single line that jumped.

An illustrative example: supplies were $8,000 last month and $10,000 this month. That's a 25% increase ($2,000 divided by $8,000). Bulk order? Price increase? Busier month? Your bookkeeper may not know why, but they can point it out so you can find out.

### What does cash look like?

Profit and cash aren't the same thing. Ask how much cash you have, what's coming due, and whether anything looks off. A practice can show a profit and still have a tight month when insurance payments run slow.

### Anything I should raise with my CPA?

Bookkeepers often spot things with tax implications, like a big equipment purchase or a change in how the owner gets paid. Ask them to flag those, and bring them to your CPA during the year rather than saving them all for spring.

## Read one page every month

You don't have to read every report. Ask for a one-page summary with the key numbers and a few sentences of explanation, or keep them on a [CFO dashboard](https://ariapractice.com/cfo-hub) you can check any time. Ten minutes a month catches most problems early.

## Make next month's close easier

At the end of each close, ask one more question: what slowed you down this month? Missing receipts, a late statement, a confusing vendor? Fix one thing each month and the close will keep getting faster. That's the real payoff of steady [bookkeeping](https://ariapractice.com/bookkeeping).
