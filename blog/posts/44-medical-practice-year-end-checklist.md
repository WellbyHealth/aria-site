A year-end financial checklist for your medical practice saves you from the January scramble. December is busy enough with patients trying to use their benefits before deductibles reset, and the last thing you want is to discover in February that three months of card transactions were never categorized.

Use this list in November and December. Some items need a few minutes. Others need a meeting. Very few can wait until spring.

## Close the books cleanly through December

Clean books make everything else on this list easier.

- **Reconcile every bank and card account** through the most recent statement, and plan to do December as soon as its statements arrive.
- **Categorize every transaction.** Clear out the "uncategorized" and "ask my accountant" buckets.
- **Match receipts** to transactions, especially larger purchases.
- **Review accounts receivable.** Look at old patient and payer balances and decide what still needs follow-up.
- **Clear credit balances.** Research and refund what's owed before the year ends.
- **Review accounts payable.** Know which bills are open and when they're due.
- **Apply your write-off policy** to balances that meet it, and document each one.

## Get payroll and contractor forms ready

Year-end forms have firm deadlines. For W-2s and Form 1099-NEC, the IRS deadline for furnishing forms has generally been January 31, but confirm current dates and requirements at [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov) or with your accountant.

- **Verify employee information**: names, addresses and Social Security numbers.
- **Review the year's payroll** for missing items, such as bonuses or taxable benefits.
- **Collect W-9s** from every contractor you paid this year, if you don't already have them.
- **List contractor payments** by vendor so your accountant can see who may need a 1099.
- **Confirm your payroll provider's year-end cutoff dates.**

## Meet with your accountant before December 31

This is the most valuable item on the list, and it only works if it happens before the year ends. Ask your accountant to walk through:

- Where the year is landing on profit
- Estimated tax payments and whether anything is still due
- Equipment or other purchases you're considering, and how timing affects them
- Retirement plan contributions and their deadlines
- Anything that changed this year, like a new location, partner or loan

Bring a current profit and loss statement and balance sheet. Don't make purchases just for tax reasons without talking it through first.

## Count supplies and update your asset list

- **Count medical and office supplies** if your accountant needs year-end inventory figures.
- **Update your fixed asset list** with equipment bought or disposed of this year, along with purchase dates and costs.
- **Save the documentation** for each major purchase.

## Review payer contracts and fee schedules

Many fee schedules update at the start of the year. Medicare's physician fee schedule, for example, is updated annually, and commercial payers often adjust rates on their own schedules.

- **List contract renewal dates** and note any coming up in the next six months.
- **Get new fee schedules** as payers publish them, and load them into your billing system.
- **Note payers that underpaid** or denied heavily this year. That's useful for renegotiation.

## Plan for slower cash in early January

Many health plans reset deductibles at the start of the year, which often means patients owe more out of pocket in the first months. Plan for that:

- Update your estimate process for the new year.
- Remind staff about collecting at the time of service.
- Make sure your cash cushion covers a slower stretch.

## Check renewals and deadlines

- Malpractice and business insurance renewals
- Professional licenses and registrations, which vary by state and credential
- Your lease and major service contracts
- Software subscriptions you no longer use (cancel them)

## Look back at the year's numbers

Take an hour to review:

- Revenue by payer and by month
- Days in accounts receivable
- Denial patterns
- Your biggest expense categories and how they changed
- No-show trends

Then set a simple budget for the coming year based on what you see.

## Tidy records and access

- Archive this year's financial records in one secure place.
- Destroy records past your retention schedule, securely.
- Review who has access to bank accounts, cards and software, and remove anyone who has left.

## Year-end is easier when it's routine

If your monthly close is solid, year-end is mostly a review. If it isn't, this checklist is a good place to start fixing that for next year.
