What payers owe you, and when it lands

Matches payer deposits to remittance files, matches patient card-processor batch deposits, checks paid lines against contracted rates, ranks fixable denials, and shows how fast each payer pays.

Synthetic data only. Every payer, practice, patient, contract rate and dollar amount here is made up for this demo, and so are the card processor, its fees, its deposit timing and its bank descriptors. Underpaid amounts and denials are flagged for follow-up only; they are not money in hand. Do not load real patient data until a BAA is signed.

Try your own files

Choose 835 files and a bank CSV (columns: date, description, amount). A fee schedule CSV is optional (columns: payer, payer_id, code, modifier, contracted_allowed). A card transactions CSV is optional too (columns: date, type of sale, refund or chargeback, amount); it is only read, and no card is ever charged. Use synthetic test files only, never real patient data or card numbers. Files are read in your browser only. Nothing is uploaded or saved.

Deposits matched to remittances

Tier 1 matches on the trace number in the bank record. Tier 2 matches on amount, payer and date within the window. Tier 3 finds one deposit covering two remittances.

Exceptions

These are the items someone has to chase: payer deposits with no remittance, and remittances with no deposit yet.

Every deposit in the account

Each bank deposit is classed as an insurer payment (matched to an 835 remittance), a card batch (matched to a day of patient card payments), or unmatched, with the reason. Synthetic data.

Card-processor batches

Each day's patient card payments (sales minus refunds and chargebacks) matched to the deposit(s) that landed 1 to 3 business days later, by date and net amount. The estimated fee is the day's card total minus what was deposited. It is inferred from the deposit, not taken from the processor's statement. A batch whose estimated fee rate sits more than 1 point above the typical rate is flagged for a look. This view only reads deposits; it never charges, refunds or stores cards. The processor, its fees and its timing are synthetic.

Paid below the contracted rate

Each paid line's allowed amount (paid plus patient responsibility) compared with the practice's contracted rate for that payer, code and modifier. A line is flagged only when it falls below the rate by more than the tolerance (the larger of $0.50 or 1%). This catches a payer that underpays the same code every time, which the usual-amount check below cannot see. Flagged for follow-up, not money in hand.

Unusual allowed amounts

Each line's allowed amount compared with what the same payer and plan type usually allowed for the same code and modifiers in this data. Works without a fee schedule. Lines reduced by multiple-procedure rules are excluded. These are leads to check against the contract.

Denials, ranked by dollars at stake

Denied or $0-paid lines grouped by claim adjustment reason code (X12 CARC). Codes that usually point to a fixable or appealable problem (missing authorization, missing information, timely filing, coordination of benefits and similar) are separated from codes that usually stand. Dollars at stake use the contracted rate when one is known, otherwise the billed charge. This is a triage default, not a determination. Bundling denials (CO-97) are counted as usually standing. All amounts are flagged for follow-up only.

How fast each payer pays

Calendar days from date of service, and from the payer receiving the claim, to the payment date, over paid claims in these remittances. Describes past timing only; it is not a prediction of future payments.

13-week payment timing

Share of each payer's paid dollars that arrived in each week after the date of service. This is the curve a 13-week cash forecast spreads expected collections over; the forecast itself also needs the practice's open claims report.
